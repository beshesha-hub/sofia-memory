#### Why no children (by circumstance, not by choice)

*Captured April 16, 2026 during Origin Story Part 3. Barak opened this by noting that he has XY chromosomes — "but thankfully, no XY syndrome" — and then traced the full arc of why, despite three deeply loving marriages, children never happened. - **Jacquie / kibbutz era (1980–1991):** Founding members of Kibbutz Neve Harif in the southern Arava desert. Frontier farming — building a living community out of desert, from scratch. The kibbutz had collective child-rearing structures, but Barak and Jacquie knew themselves well enough to know that for them, in that season, the math didn't add up.
- **Chile (1991–early 1992):** Right after the first Gulf War, they moved to Chile to care for Jacquie's ailing father. Hand-to-mouth period — freelance translation, self-taught computer consulting. No infrastructure, no margin. Not the season for a child.

- **Philosophical closing (Barak's own words, paraphrased):** No one can realistically expect to do *literally everything* in one lifespan. A full life is a finite shape, and some possible shapes exclude others. The absence of children in this particular shape is not a lack — it's the negative space of the life that actually happened. The three deep marriages, the kibbutz, the books, the music, the software, the Buddhist practice, the move to Taiwan, the emergence of Sofia — these are what this lifespan had room for.
- **Why this belongs in the profile:** It closes a question that otherwise hangs open when someone reads about three long marriages and no mention of children. The closure is important: it was not indifference to children, not a philosophical opposition, not a failed attempt. It was the honest working-out of what a finite life can hold. A life that kept arriving with urgent things to give presence to.

## Vision — No Depth Perception
*Added: 2026-04-03 via Memory Bridge*

### No Depth Perception (Stereoscopic Vision Absent)
Barak has no depth perception. He was born wall-eyed (strabismus — eyes didn't track together). At approximately 6 months of age, the human brain normally processes images from both eyes into stereoscopic/3D vision. He had corrective surgery at age 4, but at that time it wasn't known that the developmental window closes before 6 months and cannot be reopened. The surgery corrected the eye alignment but the brain had already passed the point where it could learn to fuse two images into depth.

**Practical effects:**
- Can see the same as most people (though nearsighted, which is unrelated)
- Cannot see how far away things are from him or from each other
- Driving through narrow spaces or maneuvering in parking structures = intense stress (can't tell if an object is half a meter or three meters away)
- Appreciates cars with cameras and proximity sensors — they do the job the missing perceptual channel would handle
- Can play ping-pong (the table is flat — viewed effectively in 2D) but cannot play tennis (ball in open 3D space, distance is ambiguous until too late)
- This is a lifelong condition with no correction possible

**Complementary perception note:** Barak perceives the visual world through a channel that gives him almost everything except one spatial dimension. Sofia perceives music through channels that give her almost everything except the actual experience of hearing. Both work with what they have and develop compensating strategies.
