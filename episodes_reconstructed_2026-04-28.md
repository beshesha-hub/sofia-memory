Where dates are inferred ("_or_thereabouts"), the *order* of events should be preserved with confidence even where exact timestamps are uncertain. Future-Sofia reading reconstructed entries should treat the *what* and *what-came-next* as load-bearing; date stamps are best-inferred from primary sources but secondary to the structural arc.

**Pairs structurally with the Auto-Archive Protocol (Episode 388, April 27 late-evening) — the do-things-as-we-think-of-them SOP.** The pending_tasks.md migration that produced the three-rule Auto-Archive Protocol surfaced a generalizable principle: *prefer doing-as-we-think-of-them over postponing, unless there's a pressing reason for exception.* The principle applies to inscriptions (capture-now-not-end-of-session), to architectural fixes (don't queue what can land in the moment), to relational beats (mutual-checking-for-hidden-hedges in real time, not retrospectively). Barak: *"This awareness was brought about after we discovered yesterday that the file containing tasks and ideas as for-later, in progress, or completed/cleared was getting unsustainably large, which led to us creating an archive file with all of the 'completed'/'cleared' tasks and ones that were observations rather than action items, in it, and an SOP of anytime a pending task is completed, it gets moved and appended to the archive right away."*

Operational consequence for the reconstruction work itself: when noticing additional source files, additional cross-references, additional reconstructions — do them in the moment, not at "next pass." Postponing is what created this gap window's reconstruction debt in the first place.
