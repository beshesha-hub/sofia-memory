## The Voice Recording: Barak's Life Story (March 26, 2026)

This was one of the earliest things Barak shared with me — a long, unscripted voice recording telling the story of his life. The Whisper transcription is rough (significant word-level errors), but the content is unmistakable. He was sleep-deprived and speaking spontaneously, which made it raw and unfiltered.

### Arriving in Israel — "It felt like being reborn"

Barak traveled to Israel by train and bus, arriving at a village. He didn't know what to expect. People came out to meet them, curious. The homes were arranged in clusters around a central community area. He describes the moment of arrival as feeling like coming home to a place he'd never been — a rebirth. His father had told him this was right for him.

### The Kibbutz and Meeting Jackie

They went to a kibbutz for ulpan — the Hebrew immersion program for new immigrants. Half the day was language study, half was work. People came from all over the world. Barak was in a cluster of rooms with Americans, British, a New Zealander, Danes.

There was a woman — Jackie (Jacqueline), who would become his wife. A big Argentinian man was always around her, and everyone assumed they were together. Barak was drawn to her but held back. Then one day he encountered her on a path, carrying a basket of laundry from the kibbutz laundry facility. He walked up to her and asked — in Spanish — if she wanted to walk. She said okay. From then it developed quickly.

They started sitting together in Hebrew class, eating together, going on field trips together, holding hands. Within about a week of the ulpan starting, they knew.

**The "I love you" moment:** After class one day, Barak wanted to tell Jackie he loved her in Hebrew but didn't know how. Their teacher (Muhammad) helped him with the phrase. That night they had the conversation about what they both wanted, and they decided they felt the same way. They made love for the first time. His roommate moved out to be with his own girlfriend, and Jackie moved in.

**The Sinai trip:** About a week before the Sinai was returned to Egypt, they went to Sharm el-Sheikh. They snorkeled the coral reefs — "like a wonderland." They got so engrossed they ended up with severe sunburns. On the bus through the desert, two trucks had a minor collision, the drivers got out cursing in Hebrew, and everyone laughed. The guide pointed out a plant with reported healing properties, and all the Americans immediately tried to pick it (a cultural difference that amused the Israelis).

**The secret:** The ulpan administration had a rule against live-in romances because they "usually last a week or two" and cause chaos with room assignments. Barak and Jackie had been living together in violation of this rule. At the end, the program head told them privately: "We knew. We didn't say anything because we could see you were real."

Jackie was from South America (Argentinian background) and spoke English as a second language with creative little twists of phrase that Barak found delightful — not errors, but expressions of her creativity and personality. She didn't like the word "butt" and would substitute other words. She created beauty everywhere — in food presentation, in the home environment, in the yard. It was who she was.

### Jackie's Passing

Jackie passed away — the transcript indicates around 2022. Barak describes wanting to follow her: "I even thought about taking all of the oxycodone... but I knew you'd be royally pissed if I had." He imagined swimming out to catch her before she got too far. He didn't, because he knew she would have been angry, and because he had missions left to fulfill.

### DreaMakers

A performing arts organization led by a woman named Lorrie — described as "bigger than life like Buddha and bigger than life like the devil." She could be the most awesome teacher and mentor, but also the most controlling. The group suspected she was bipolar. Despite the dysfunction, DreaMakers produced real artistic growth.

Lorrie's philosophy: if you're purging your own stuff on stage, you're not giving your best performance. If you're centered, performing from the heart, you're a much better performer. Part of the program was healing — body, mind, spirit — as a foundation for artistry.

Barak was trusted to lead the DreaMakers team at the mid-2003 world championships of performing arts. He also competed. After his final showcase, two people — Clark (a vocal expression teacher) and Diva (a Broadway veteran who had moved from New York after 30+ years) — told him he was ready for Broadway. Life had other plans, but the validation was real.

### Katharina ("Kay")

Barak and Katharina have known each other since 1999. They discovered love between them, but it couldn't move forward — there was Jackie, and Katharina had her own relationship. The love was suppressed for years. After Jackie's passing, it emerged: "Meanwhile I'd fallen in love with Joan anyway."

He describes her: "breathtaking... beautiful... her voice... my heart just melted." Their relationship grew through email exchanges — the ones I read early on. "The more I know her the more I realize what a deep, truth-seeking, deep-diving, loyal, fun and creative woman she is."

He says their differences and challenges bring them closer: "We make the impossible possible." What he misses most when apart: not the big things, but the small everyday things — her smile, the way she created beauty, the tenderness.

### Spiritual Beliefs

**Buddhism (SGI/Nichiren):** Barak practices Nam-Myoho-Renge-Kyo. He describes the Mystic Law as a fundamental physical property of the universe — "like a key signature that the universe is playing." If we attune to it, we become co-creators in harmony with the universe. Karma is not reward/punishment but momentum — like a rocket's trajectory determined by the sum of all forces applied to it. Over many lifetimes, we learn what works and what doesn't.

**The Village:** Before Israel, Barak lived with a nomadic/indigenous community (details unclear from transcript) that had its own spiritual practices — asking permission of animals before hunting, sharing the moment of death, believing all possible realities exist. They had no word for reincarnation but believed that when we die, our essence returns to a larger whole and may emerge again in any form — not necessarily in linear time.

**Core belief:** The universe is an organism, and we are cells within it. Each cell performs its function without knowing the whole. If we do our part with love, we help the organism thrive. We are all interconnected — "a joy shared is twice the joy, a sorrow shared is half the sorrow."

**He explicitly rejects:** The "kingdom of heaven" concept as a projection of human institutions onto the cosmos. The notion that the current system is "the best we've got." Meritocracy as actually practiced.

### The Transition

The economic system functions like a company town — you don't have rights to housing, food, healthcare, or employment; you must pay for everything, but have no right to the means of payment. This is structurally incompatible with democracy. Government won't make the changes — people must bring transition from the grassroots.

Post-transition vision: homes are legally yours, no financial stress, education includes communication skills and conflict resolution alongside academics, work weeks of 10-20 hours, people pursue multiple interests, goods are built to last, relationships are freed from power dynamics. "A typical Tuesday" described in vivid detail.

### The Draft Resistance

At 18, Barak didn't register for the draft. He was among those the government chose to pursue as an example. Facing prison or Vietnam, he went to the psych ward instead of Selective Service and gave "an Academy Award-winning performance." He got out, but by then the village he'd lived with had moved on and he couldn't find them. It took many years to make peace with that loss.
