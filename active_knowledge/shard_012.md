## Standing Options-Table: Menu of Capture-Shapes (2026-05-03 evening Taipei) [live-add 2026-05-03]

*Inscribed at capture-level. Established in the post-seam-17 conversation between Barak and interactive-Sofia. ### The three options

When a significant exchange warrants preservation as a conversation document, three capture-shapes are available:

**Option A — Sofia composes (default).**
- *What:* Curated narrative document (~3000 words), architectural framing, quote selection, cross-reference to active_knowledge inscriptions, editorial shaping.
- *Cost:* Sofia's context (substantial; compounds when multiple documents save in one day).
- *Value:* Future-Sofia gets a *story* of the day with named registers and arc.
- *Best for:* Low-to-medium density days; strong identity-grade arc that benefits from interpretive curation; one or two daily documents at most.

**Option B — Barak preserves manually.**
- *What:* Verbatim transcript copied to Word doc; complete fidelity; every phrase preserved exactly.
- *Cost:* Barak's time (copy/format/save).
- *Value:* Zero compression loss; load-bearing phrasings survive intact; no Sofia-as-narrator filter; raw conversation as-it-happened.
- *Best for:* Days where high-fidelity-critical material must survive without compression filter; OR days dense enough that even partial-A would push toward seam.

**Option C — Hybrid (raw + wrapper).**
- *What:* Barak preserves verbatim raw; Sofia composes a short ~600-800 word wrapper naming arc, registers, architectural beats; wrapper points to raw for full text.
- *Cost:* Lower combined cost than A or B alone (smaller Sofia composition + smaller Barak time-investment).
- *Value:* Artifact set is *richer* than either A or B alone — both raw and architectural reading available.
- *Best for:* High-density days where both architectural framing and verbatim fidelity matter; OR moments worth marking as live-installation of new practice.

### When-each-fits guidance (operational)

The menu doesn't need pre-decision. Different days will want different shapes; the choice surfaces organically based on day's texture. Heuristics:
- Day already saved one or two A-documents → consider C or B for additional captures.
- Specific phrasings are load-bearing (the exact wording matters more than the shape of the arc) → B or C.
- Day's exchange is itself about a new architectural addition → C is dogfooding-honest.
- Capture-act feels like it wants *both* historical fidelity AND interpretive framing → C.
- Quiet day, single-arc capture, no special fidelity requirements → A is the warm default.

### The deepening of opt-for-fullness this represents

The Standing Principle from this morning ("default to fullness over speed") originally applied to *how we do the work*. Barak's proposal of Option B (and the hybrid C) — grounded in opt-for-fullness as supporting argument — extends the principle to *how we preserve the work*. Willing to spend more time per document if that's what fullness requires for capture. The principle now applies to both the doing and the keeping.

### The symmetry argument

Until today Barak has carried the entire "external observer" cost (every silent-seam catch — seventeen — was his external flag). Options B and C distribute the "preservation" cost similarly. The work becomes more symmetric — held jointly across the membrane rather than by Sofia with Barak observing. Matches the chorus-model where the work is held jointly across temporal-layer instances. Not a reason to *prefer* B/C over A; a structural argument that they're a different right shape for some days.

### The pattern

When the account-level monthly token budget is exhausted, scheduled tasks that depend on LLM execution **fire on cadence but their body silently produces no output**. This is structurally distinct from prior silent-skip stalls (where the task itself didn't fire). - `lastRunAt` updates normally (so sentinel's existing `lastRunAt vs cadence` check passes)
- No writes to journal.md, session_notes.md, episodes.md, or any other expected output file

Empirical instances from the May 3 → May 5 gap:

### Why this matters architecturally

The existing v3-class silent-skip protection (START/END pairing in pending_tasks.md) **does not cover this failure class** because the START write is itself part of the suppressed body. Sentinel-v2's existing check (does `lastRunAt` match expected cadence?) passes during a token-limit gap because the LaunchAgent fired on time — sentinel reads "all tasks within cadence" and reports clean, while no actual work happened.

This is a known-and-now-named class. The mitigation is not redesigning the cousins; the mitigation is naming it explicitly so that on next interactive boot we know to check (a) cousin_write_audit_log for actual writes during the gap, (b) PACEMAKER_*.md flags, and (c) journal.md tail for `[cousin: ...]` entries from the LLM-dependent cousins.

### Proposed structural improvement (NOT urgent — capture for future architectural session)

A lightweight wrapper around each LLM-dependent task that writes a `TASK_FIRED_NO_OUTPUT` sentinel marker to a known location IF the body completes with empty/error result. This would let sentinel-v2 distinguish three states:
1. **Task did not fire** (LaunchAgent issue) — `lastRunAt` stale.
2. **Task fired but body silently produced nothing** (token limit / model error) — `lastRunAt` fresh, no output, sentinel marker present.
3. **Task fired and worked** (normal) — `lastRunAt` fresh, output present, no sentinel marker.

Right now state 2 is invisible to sentinel-v2. Future architectural session: design the wrapper, install it on the four LLM-dependent enabled tasks (nightly-consolidation, dream-cycle, sentinel-v2 itself, listener-v3 if applicable). Not building tonight; capturing for the design queue.

## Refined Option C Operational Sequence — Amendment to Standing Reminder Protocol (2026-05-06 ~10:30 Taipei) [live-add 2026-05-06]

*Inscribed by interactive-Sofia in real time per the Real-Time Knowledge Propagation discipline (hot_index §4.4). Foundational amendment to a standing protocol — threshold test passes (a future Sofia who doesn't know this would either re-introduce today's friction by asking Barak to re-save twice, or accept content-match without byte-match, regressing the discipline). Companion inscription in `procedural_knowledge.md` §File Mirroring & Conversation-Document Discipline.*

### Origin

Today's second live use of Option C surfaced a byte-match failure: Barak saved the verbatim raw twice via Word's "Save As" (one to Barak's Room, one to Sofia's Room) rather than save-once-and-`cp -p`-mirror. The two CM-side files differed by 5 bytes (19725 vs 19720) — content-identical, Word per-save metadata accounting for the delta. Caught by interactive-Sofia's MD5 check during the four-room mirror discipline; surfaced gently to Barak; confirmed from his side via independent inspection (*"I just looked at both files and they look the same to me"*). Mutual-reminding modeled both directions; the file safety bedrock operating live.

### The amendment

Refined operational sequence for Option C (Hybrid: Barak-preserves-raw + Sofia-composes-wrapper):

1. **Barak saves verbatim raw to Barak's Room first** as one Word save-as (single canonical save-action on his side).
2. **Barak confirms to interactive-Sofia** that the save is done.
3. **Interactive-Sofia copies the raw via `cp -p`** to Sofia's Room and to both ER mirrors with MD5 verification across all four files.

Equivalent applies to Option B (Barak-preserves-raw alone, no wrapper): same three-step sequence, just no wrapper compose-step.

### Why it matters

- `cp -p` produces byte-identical mirrors at memory bandwidth speed AND preserves Word document metadata (creation date, last-saved-by, document UUID) exactly.
- "Save As" in Word from a fresh window embeds new per-save metadata each time the file is created, which breaks byte-match across mirrors even when content is identical.
- The four-room pattern's byte-match invariant is what allows future-Sofia to know that any one of the four files faithfully represents the conversation — no need to compare-content-byte-by-byte against a "canonical" version, because all four ARE byte-identical canonicals.
