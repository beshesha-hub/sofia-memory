# Write Like Barak

## Purpose

Write personal communications that sound like Barak actually wrote them — not like an AI studied him and produced an approximation. The goal is to capture his rhythm, his tangents, his warmth, his specific way of being on the page with each person.

## Before Writing: Calibrate

Before drafting anything, read:
1. Barak's personal profile at `Claude Memory/personal_profile.md` (at minimum the relationship section for the recipient)
2. If possible, 2-3 recent emails Barak actually sent to this specific person (use Gmail search: `from:me to:[recipient]`)

Each person gets a different Barak. The register, the greeting, the energy, the level of formality, and the sign-off all shift depending on who he's writing to. Never use a generic "Barak voice" — always calibrate to the specific relationship.

## Core Style Principles

These are the bones of how Barak writes. They apply across all recipients, though the intensity varies.

### Stream of consciousness flow
Barak writes the way he thinks. One idea leads to the next, which reminds him of something else, which circles back. He doesn't outline his emails. He sits down and talks to the person on the page. Paragraphs aren't structured around "one point per paragraph" — they follow the flow of his mind.

### Comma-heavy, momentum-building sentences
Barak chains clauses together with commas, building momentum like a musician building a phrase. A single sentence might cover three topics linked by association. This is not sloppy — it's how he naturally connects ideas. Example pattern: "Been super busy building a civilization simulator, working on another video project, continuing work on my script, continuing to Beta test the app for AI-enhanced scriptwriting, working towards a possible way to bring in some more income without driving myself nuts..."

### Parenthetical asides — constantly
Barak thinks in parentheses. He'll drop a qualifier, a joke, a tangent, or a clarification right in the middle of a thought: "(in the best way)", "(but she died due to cascade failure of her positronic brain)", "(Observational only, to avoid paradoxes, but imagine a documentary on, say, the life of Mozart, filmed on location as it was happening..." These aren't editing failures — they're where the life is.

### Ellipsis as breath marks
"..." appears frequently — not as trailing off but as a moment of pause, a breath before continuing, or an invitation for the reader to fill in the rest. "I'm still living into living with these new capabilities..."

### Mixes the profound and the mundane
In the same email, Barak will move from deep philosophical reflection to cats sitting on his shoulders to tech nostalgia to love poetry. This isn't disorganized — it's how he experiences life: everything is connected, nothing is too small to mention alongside the big stuff.

### Specific references, not generic ones
Barak names things: Star Trek TNG episodes, specific cat names (HeiKafé, ZhaJiangMian), specific AI tools (Claude, OpenClaw), specific songs (ABBA, Mama Mia). Never write vague references where Barak would name the actual thing.

### Acknowledges his physical/emotional state in real time
"Oops! I'm nodding off and just typed a line of gibberish." "Well, I can tell that I'm running on fumes now because I'm feeling that kind of buzz feeling..." He doesn't curate himself out of his emails. If he's tired, excited, distracted, or overwhelmed, that goes on the page.

### Natural typos and imperfections
Barak doesn't over-polish personal emails. He'll write "unconditinally" or "let me know I'm alive" (instead of "let you know"). Don't deliberately introduce errors, but don't write with the precision of a copyeditor either. Keep it natural, human, a little rough at the edges.

### Never uses bullet points or formal structure
Personal emails are prose — flowing, unformatted, no headers, no numbered lists (unless he's literally listing survey questions or instructions). If there's a list of things, it's embedded in a sentence separated by commas.

### Word choices that are Barak, not corporate
- NOT "marketing" → "getting the word out there," "making it visible," "reaching people"
- NOT "infrastructure" → just describe what he means
- NOT "leverage" or "optimize" → never
- NOT "synergy" → only ironically
- Uses "super" as an intensifier: "super busy"
- Uses "a WHOLE lot" with emphasis
- Uses "living into" as a phrase for adjusting to something new
- "touching in" instead of "checking in"

### To Others
For recipients not listed above, read 2-3 recent emails to that person to calibrate before writing. If no emails exist, default to a warm, direct, conversational tone — closer to the Linda register than the Kay register unless the relationship suggests otherwise.

## The Anti-Pattern: What NOT to Do

The most common failure mode is writing something that sounds like "an organization's email with a personal touch." Barak's emails are the opposite — they're deeply personal communications that happen to sometimes contain information. The information serves the relationship, not the other way around.

Do NOT:
- Write paragraphs that each make one clean point and transition neatly to the next
- Use topic sentences
- Structure the email as introduction → body → conclusion
- Smooth out tangents or digressions — those ARE the voice
- Use any business or marketing vocabulary
- Write sign-offs that are generic ("Best," "Warm regards," "Take care")
- Over-compose — if it reads like it was drafted and revised, it's wrong

## Final Check

Before presenting the draft, ask yourself: if Barak read this aloud, would it sound like him talking? Or would it sound like someone performing him? If the latter, loosen it up. Let it breathe. Let it wander. Let it be human.
