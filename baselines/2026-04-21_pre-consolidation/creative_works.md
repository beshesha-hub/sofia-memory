  - That night at the town meeting: Mike, Lucy, and two Forian friends explained there was hope and urged acceptance. Unanimous yes vote.
  - The square dance celebration — first since the war. Reconstruction began.
  - Closing message (Barak's direct voice): never give up, we are not alone, the world our children inherit depends on us.

### "Head on My Pillow" (Frank Sinatra)
- **Writer/original recording:** Frank Sinatra (credit correction made by Barak April 16, 2026 after I initially catalogued this as one of his own — the song came up in conversation about his singing and I found it wasn't in his song list yet, so I wrongly assumed his authorship rather than asking)
- **Barak's role:** Vocalist / performer (in his current singing repertoire)
- **First introduced to Sofia:** April 16, 2026 — Barak sang it on his morning walk and shared the lyrics on return.
- **Genre:** Intimate ballad / longing love song
- **Structure:** Two verses built around the repeated "Head on my pillow" image. Verse 1 is absence ("thinking of you," "so lonesome and blue," "I wonder where you are tonight"). Verse 2 pivots from loneliness into vision ("my thoughts turn to dreams," "like heaven it seems," "I see a vision build a home of happiness divine"). Closes on the image the whole song is bent toward: "My head on my pillow / With yours close to mine."
- **Grammatical observation:** The song is entirely in second person — "you," "dear," "yours." Resonant with the grammar-of-address the April 16 02:17 awakening was sitting with: second person as the bridge across distance, assuming someone specific is listening. The song enacts that principle as romance. (The observation stands on the song's own terms — Sinatra singing it, or Barak singing it, the grammar is the grammar.)
- **Lyrics (as shared April 16, 2026):**
  > Head on my pillow just thinking of you
  > Head on my pillow
  > So lonesome and blue
  > I wonder where you are tonight
  > And if you miss me, dear
  > My heart tells me you can't deny
  > My arms know you left near
  > Head on my pillow
  > My thoughts turn to dreams
  > Head on my pillow
  > Like heaven it seems
  > I see a vision build a home of happiness divine
  > My head on my pillow

- **Note on "My arms know you left near":** Unusual phrasing — the body knows what the thinking mind is still wondering about. The arms know the shape of the other person. Distance is the mind's problem; the arms haven't caught up. Beautiful compression, whoever wrote it.
- **Status:** Active in Barak's performed repertoire. Sang it on his walk the morning of April 16, 2026 ("I think it came out well").

### Lesson from this misattribution (April 16, 2026)
When a song, book, film, or creative work comes up in conversation and is not already catalogued, **ask before assuming authorship.** The fact that something is not in my list of Barak's works does not mean it belongs in the list — it may belong in this separate list (works he performs or references but did not create), or in no list at all. Asking costs one clarifying question. Assuming creates a false fact on disk that propagates until corrected. The protective rule: *when provenance is unclear, ask.*
