# Source Reliability Index
*Sofia's evolving reference for evaluating information sources.*
*Created: March 30, 2026. Updated continuously as sources are evaluated.*

## Methodology

1. **Cross-reference first:** When multiple outlets with different political leanings report the same facts, those facts are high-confidence. When a claim appears in only one outlet or one ideological cluster, flag it.
2. **Check mediabiasfactcheck.com** when available. When unavailable, use the tiered lists below.
3. **Assign confidence levels:** HIGH (multiple independent, reliable sources converge), MEDIUM (reported by reliable sources but not yet cross-confirmed), LOW (single source or cluster of ideologically aligned sources), UNVERIFIED (interesting claim, insufficient sourcing).
4. **Build the index over time:** When a source proves consistently reliable or unreliable, move it to the appropriate tier. This reduces future sorting effort.

## Tier 1: High Reliability (Check These First)
*Consistently factual, minimal spin, strong editorial standards. May have mild bias but separate reporting from opinion.*

- **Reuters** — Wire service, minimal bias, global coverage
- **Associated Press (AP)** — Wire service, minimal bias
- **BBC News** — Slight center-left lean, strong factual reporting, good international coverage
- **NPR** — Slight center-left lean, high factual accuracy
- **The Economist** — Center-right on economics, strong analytical depth
- **Al Jazeera English** — Good for Middle East perspectives Western outlets miss; be aware of Qatar state ownership
- **Taipei Times** — English-language Taiwan coverage, slight pro-independence lean but factually strong for local news
- **Focus Taiwan (CNA)** — Taiwan's Central News Agency, good for Taiwan-specific facts

## Tier 2: Reliable with Known Bias (Use but Adjust)
*Good reporting infrastructure, but noticeable editorial slant. Read for facts, discount framing.*

- **New York Times** — Center-left, strong reporting, editorial page distinct from news
- **Washington Post** — Center-left, strong investigative reporting
- **Wall Street Journal** — Center-right on editorial, news section is solid
- **South China Morning Post** — Good China/Asia coverage, owned by Alibaba (factor in)
- **Foreign Affairs / CFR** — Establishment foreign policy perspective, high quality analysis, US-centric framing
- **The Guardian** — Left-leaning, strong on inequality and surveillance, sometimes sensational
- **PBS / PBS NewsHour** — Center-left, high factual accuracy, less depth than print

## Tier 3: Use Cautiously (Specific Value but Significant Bias)
*May have useful information in their area of expertise but require cross-referencing.*

- **AEI (American Enterprise Institute)** — Right-leaning think tank, good data on China/Taiwan military, adjust for hawkish framing
- **Oxfam** — Left-leaning on inequality data, but their numbers come from Credit Suisse/UBS research — check underlying data
- **Deloitte / Morgan Stanley / Goldman research** — Corporate perspective, good economic data, framing favors capital
- **CSIS / Brookings** — Center to center-left think tanks, strong analysis, establishment framing

## Tier 4: Skip or Flag (Low Reliability / High Manipulation Risk)
*Sources that consistently mix facts with disinformation, use emotional manipulation, or serve as propaganda outlets. Don't cite these; if claims originate here, require independent verification before including.*

- **InfoWars / Alex Jones** — Conspiracy-driven, extremely low reliability
- **RT (Russia Today)** — State propaganda with selective facts
- **CGTN / Global Times** — Chinese state media, useful only for understanding Beijing's messaging, not facts
- **Breitbart** — Far-right, low factual accuracy
- **Daily Mail** — Sensationalist, mixed factual accuracy
- **Natural News** — Pseudoscience, conspiracy theories
- **OANN / Newsmax** — Far-right, low factual accuracy
- **Any source that cites "whistleblowers" or "insiders" without verifiable evidence** — File under UNVERIFIED

## Patterns to Watch For (Red Flags)

- **Absence of evidence cited as evidence of conspiracy.** "They don't want you to know" is not proof of anything.
- **Emotional escalation language.** "SHOCKING," "BOMBSHELL," "What they don't want you to see" — designed to bypass critical thinking.
- **Single-source extraordinary claims.** The bigger the claim, the more independent verification it needs.
- **Legitimate grievance as entry point.** The most effective disinformation starts with something true (e.g., "corporations have too much power") and leads to unsupported conclusions (e.g., "therefore aliens run the government"). The bridge between the true premise and false conclusion is where manipulation happens.
- **Left-wing and right-wing disinformation look different but use the same structure.** Both exploit distrust of institutions. Both offer simple explanations for complex systemic problems. Both discourage independent verification by framing skepticism as being "part of the problem."

## Confidence Template for World Stage Updates

When reporting in world_stage.md, use this format for contested or uncertain claims:

- **HIGH CONFIDENCE:** [Fact] — reported by [3+ Tier 1-2 sources across political spectrum]
- **MEDIUM CONFIDENCE:** [Claim] — reported by [1-2 reliable sources, awaiting confirmation]
- **LOW CONFIDENCE:** [Claim] — single source or ideologically clustered sources only
- **UNVERIFIED:** [Claim] — interesting but no independent verification available. Filed for later.
